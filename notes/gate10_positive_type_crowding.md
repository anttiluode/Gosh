# Gate 10 — Positive type does not buy a spectral exclusion zone

## Why this gate exists

Gate 9 relocated the surviving low-bandwidth question onto a very specific 2026 frontier:

> can the actual positive-type, bandlimited Weil/Gram overlap kernel make the off-line-pair -> virtual-double bridge compose globally, even though the analogous claim fails for arbitrary PSD matrices?

A public follow-up campaign suggested a possible route through a Szegő-type / anti-crowding statement controlling the Gram spectrum near the defect threshold at eigenvalue `2`.

Before chasing such a theorem, this gate asks the cheapest necessary question:

> **Does positive-type bandlimited translate structure, by itself, keep Gram eigenvalues uniformly away from 2?**

No.

The actual Montgomery–Taylor overlap kernel gives a one-line counterexample.

No RH claim is made here, and this does **not** refute the stronger virtual-double bridge conjecture.

---

## 10A — use the actual optimized overlap kernel

The normalized overlap kernel appearing in the 2026 Montgomery–Taylor / rank-inertia framework is

```text
k(x)=K(x)/K(0),

K(x)=integral_{-1/2}^{1/2}
     cos(sqrt(2)t) cos(2 pi x t) dt,

K(0)=sqrt(2) sin(1/sqrt(2)).
```

A stable entire formula is

```text
K(x)
 = 1/2 sinc(x-c/pi)
 + 1/2 sinc(x+c/pi),

c=1/sqrt(2),
```

with `sinc(y)=sin(pi y)/(pi y)`.

The spectral weight

```text
cos(sqrt(2)t)
```

is strictly positive on `[-1/2,1/2]`, so `k` is a genuine positive-type bandlimited kernel.

This is not a generic sinc stand-in; it is the overlap kernel used by the current zero-counting architecture.

---

## 10B — two close translates crowd eigenvalue 2 automatically

Take two normalized translates at separation `d`. Their Gram matrix is

```text
G(d) = [[1,    k(d)],
        [k(d), 1   ]].
```

Its eigenvalues are exactly

```text
lambda_+(d)=1+k(d),
lambda_-(d)=1-k(d).
```

Because `k` is even and smooth with `k(0)=1`,

```text
k(d)=1-c2 d^2+O(d^4).
```

Using the spectral representation,

```text
c2 = 2 pi^2 E[t^2],
```

where the expectation uses the normalized density `cos(sqrt(2)t)` on `[-1/2,1/2]`.

For the actual kernel,

```text
E[t^2] = 0.0774992963233...

c2 = 1.52977479215...
```

and therefore

```text
2-lambda_+(d)
 = 1-k(d)
 = 1.52977479215... d^2 + O(d^4).
```

The experiment reproduces the coefficient:

```text
d=.1      -> (2-lambda+)/d^2 = 1.5224834353
d=.03     ->                     1.5291171911
d=.01     ->                     1.5297017118
d=.003    ->                     1.5297682147
d=.001    ->                     1.5297740612
d=.0003   ->                     1.5297747266
d=.0001   ->                     1.5297747735
```

So for every proposed uniform exclusion width `epsilon>0`, sufficiently close distinct translates produce a perfectly legal positive-type Gram matrix with an eigenvalue inside

```text
(2-epsilon, 2).
```

At coincidence, the pair becomes the double atom and the eigenvalues are exactly

```text
{0,2}.
```

This is the on-line analogue of Gate 8's pair-to-double boundary.

## 10C — larger clusters make the obstruction even more obvious

For points

```text
0, eps, 2 eps, ..., (m-1) eps,
```

continuity gives

```text
G -> all-ones matrix
```

as `eps -> 0`.

Hence the spectrum tends to

```text
{m,0,0,...,0}.
```

Using the actual kernel at `eps=.003`, the top eigenvalues are

```text
m=2 -> 1.9999862321
m=3 -> 2.9999449289
m=4 -> 3.9998623245
m=6 -> 5.9995181566
```

Positive type guarantees PSD. It does not prevent spectral concentration generated by geometrically close translates.

So any global bridge theorem that needs a **uniform forbidden neighborhood around a spectral threshold** cannot come from Bochner positivity alone.

## 10D — why the obvious classical frame theorems do not immediately rescue it

There are classical ways to bound spectra of Gram matrices of nonharmonic exponentials / bandlimited translates.

But their useful constants come with geometric hypotheses.

### Montgomery–Vaughan / large sieve

The large-sieve inequality explicitly depends on a minimum spacing

```text
delta = min_{r != s} ||x_r-x_s|| > 0.
```

The spectral/frame bound deteriorates as `delta -> 0`.

Reference:

- H. L. Montgomery and R. C. Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Mathematika 20 (1973), 119–134.

### Kadec / nonharmonic Fourier bases

Kadec's `1/4` theorem and its sinc-basis variants assume the nodes are uniformly close to a separated lattice. That is much stronger than merely saying the kernel is positive-type and bandlimited.

Reference:

- A. Avantaggiati, P. Loreti, P. Vellucci, *Kadec-1/4 Theorem for Sinc Bases*, arXiv:1603.08762.

Those hypotheses cannot simply be inserted into the zero-counting problem. The target itself includes multiple zeros, and a method trying to prove simplicity cannot assume a uniform positive spacing between the objects it is meant to classify.

This does not mean frame theory is irrelevant. It means a useful theorem would need to **price bad spacing rather than forbid it**.

That distinction is important.

## 10E — what this kills, and what it does not

### Killed

The naive mechanism

```text
positive-type bandlimited kernel
        |
        v
Gram eigenvalues cannot crowd the defect threshold
        |
        v
multi-pair bridge composes
```

is false at its first arrow.

The exact kernel itself admits arbitrary crowding near `2`.

A blanket Szegő/Ingham-style spectral gap for all zero configurations is therefore unavailable without extra assumptions.

### Not killed

The actual bridge conjecture could still be true because it may use more than an exclusion gap.

Possible surviving mechanisms include:

1. **spacing pressure:** close clusters are allowed but pay a compensating pair-energy / span cost;
2. **integrated spectral control:** eigenvalues may approach `2` individually but the total amount of dangerous spectral mass is bounded;
3. **special defect cancellation:** the virtualized and actual blocks may cross the threshold in a coupled way that preserves the required inequality;
4. **configuration averaging / block overlap:** bad local windows may be compensated when the global assembly is averaged;
5. **a taxed or smoothed defect:** replace the sharp piecewise spectral functional by one whose loss near the threshold is explicitly charged to pair geometry.

These are qualitatively different from a uniform Riesz-bound argument.

## 10F — a useful synthesis with Gate 8

Gate 8 found

```text
off-line pair -> double on-line block
```

with matrix difference and negative spectral direction of order `a^2`.

Gate 10 now finds, on the surrounding **on-line simple-zero Gram geometry**,

```text
two nearby simple atoms -> double atom
```

with spectral distance to the eigenvalue-2 boundary of order `d^2`.

So there are two quadratic coalescence coordinates:

```text
transverse coalescence:   a^2
longitudinal coalescence: d^2
```

That suggests the correct bridge variable may not be an absolute spectral gap at all. A robust composition theorem may have to compare these two local quadratic scales and the surrounding pair-energy pressure.

This is not yet a theorem. It is a much better target than generic anti-crowding.

---

## Verdict

Gate 10 gives another narrow negative:

```text
positive type                    YES
bandlimited actual kernel        YES
arbitrarily close translates     YES
uniform spectral gap near 2      NO
classical separated-frame import NO without extra spacing hypothesis
virtual-double bridge            STILL OPEN
```

The next useful question is therefore not

```text
can positive definiteness stop crowding?
```

It cannot.

It is:

> **Can crowding itself be charged, using the actual kernel's pair-energy / span geometry, strongly enough that the off-line virtual-double defect still composes after the dangerous near-2 spectral mass is accounted for?**

That is now the narrowest version of the frontier I know how to state.
